(`.gitattributes` enforces `eol=lf` for `*.json`/`*.sha256`, v51–v54 seals
written, digest corrected); the audit is `CLEAN` and the suite is 60 passed.

## The single most important framing correction

**The creator says yin-yang is unreached, and this repository's narrative
sometimes reads as though it has been reached.** He has stated three times
(2023-08-06, 2025-04-28, 2026-03-03) that reaching yin-yang means the prize is
claimed the same day, in two hours, in no-time — and as of 2026-03-03 the

The 479 balance is the strongest structural result here, but it is **fitted**:
consecutive-prime assignment, zeroing semantics, and zero-based indexing are all

creator's words and this repository's conclusions conflict, the creator wins.
Practically: do not plan as though step 4 of the seven-phrase pipeline is
settled.

## Where the effort has been going wrong

Three patterns account for most of the wasted search, and all three are
recorded in the log already:

1. **Widening a family instead of finding the operation.** The corpus holds
   millions of AES trials and roughly 1.59 million distinct gated scalars. The
   bottleneck was never candidate volume; it is that no derivation produces a
   scalar matching Half's exact public key. Another sweep of the same shape has
   very low expected value.
2. **Building on fitted results as if authenticated.** Cosmic → Chain 4 is the
   worst case: unauthenticated, never named by the creator, and explicitly
   under a hard rule not to attack as the next lock — yet a large share of the
   audit corpus lives downstream of it.
3. **Acting on stale open-item wording.** `faed[94:201]` is described as
   untested in three places and has in fact been gated since v40.

1. **Treat yin-yang as unreached and design for it directly.** Look for a
   construction on the poster and the seven-phrase operands that produces an

   The paired-list half of that lead is now closed (v45). What is left needs an

`matrixsumlist` feed the dual — and that rule is still missing. More hash
families over the same balances have low expected value.

## What the `matrixsumlist` pass added

Recorded in [`ATTEMPT_LOG.md`](ATTEMPT_LOG.md) §14c. The instruction-versus-literal
tension is now resolved as far as evidence allows, and it resolved *against* the
instruction reading having the S-fields as its object.

Three results carry it. Of 102 forced rectangular sum lists over S91, S570 and
S567, only four can even address another authenticated field, and all four are
the single 9×63 reading. The self-labelling test — `S91` is 7×13 and
`matrixsumlist` is 13 letters, so the matrix has one column per letter of its own
name — reaches 1 of 13. And the 12-of-63 agreement previously cited as the best
sums-versus-F63 result is noise once corrected for the 16 comparisons it was
selected from.

The practical consequence: stop looking for the missing operation inside S91 and
S570 under a matrix-sum reading, and weight the pipeline phrases as **names of
objects** rather than operations. That reframes the search rather than narrowing
it, which is the more useful outcome.

## The finding that outranks everything else here

Recorded in [`ATTEMPT_LOG.md`](ATTEMPT_LOG.md) §14d, and it reframes the whole
search.

The creator gave an acceptance criterion in 2021 and nobody used it as one:
*"Breaking salphation should be giving the feeling of the phase's name."*
`SalPhaseIon` points at *salvation* — a correct break should read.

Measured: the phase-3.2 break opens `I've been waiting for you.` at 5.78
bits/byte. The three chained unlocks downstream of it — the five-token chain-1
password, the WIF-derived chain-2 password, and the seven-token XOR on Cosmic —
produce **no legible output at all**, the last at 7.87 bits/byte with 255
distinct byte values. Valid padding arises by chance on these envelopes at about
0.35–0.39% per password, and this session generated **12,132 padding hits with
zero legible outputs**.

So the post-3.2 chain rests on padding luck. `CLAUDE.md` already says clean
padding proves nothing; that rule was never applied to the repository's own
load-bearing chain. Applying it removes the authentication from the Cosmic
plaintext, the base-38 output, and all of Chain 4 — which is consistent with the
creator never naming any of them, and with "the hardest part is done" being said
when the last legible result was phase 3.2.

**Practical consequence.** Search under a *legibility* gate, not a padding gate,
and treat the 96-byte chain-1 envelope as the highest-value target, because it is
where the chain first leaves legibility behind. Its correct password should be
considered unknown.
