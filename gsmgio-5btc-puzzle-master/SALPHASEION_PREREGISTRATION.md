# SalPhaseIon source-only preregistration

This experiment is sealed before any candidate decryption. It deliberately does
not import or consult expected padding lengths, `+/-`, Half/Better Half, Chain 4,
the prize point/address, or hashes of alleged community plaintexts.

## Frozen source

The source of truth is the earliest cached Wayback capture, timestamp
`20230601222752`. The first and second textarea bodies are identical byte for
byte in all five cached captures from 2023 through 2026. HTML framing differences
are irrelevant.

The mechanically decoded first-textarea fields occur in this physical order:

1. `matrixsumlist`
2. `lastwordsbeforearchichoice`
3. `thispassword`
4. `shabefourfirsthintisyourlastcommand`
5. `enter` (embedded between the two Base64 portions)
6. `shabefanstoo`

This order is frozen. In particular, it is not the community password order.
The lowercase `z` after `...efvdrd9` is retained as the 64th Base64 character.

## Candidate constructions

Four finite families are registered:

1. Every contiguous run of the six decoded fields in physical source order,
   serialized either compactly or with one ASCII space between fields.
2. The Matrix Reloaded line prompted by `lastwordsbeforearchichoice` is pinned as
   “As you adequately put, the problem is choice.” Every suffix of the seven
   words before `choice` is registered, compact and space-separated. Registering
   all suffixes prevents choosing how many “last words” to keep after decryption.
3. Literal row, column, row-then-column, column-then-row, and total sum lists for
   S91 as 7x13 and 13x7, and S570 as 19x30 and 30x19. Both page-authenticated
   letter mappings are registered: a=1..i=9 and a=0..i=8. Each list uses exactly
   four predetermined serializations: concatenated decimal, spaces, commas, and
   compact JSON.
4. The exact authenticated access loop: `HASHTHETEXT`, the access preimage, and
   its lowercase SHA-256 hash.

Each unique preimage has exactly two password forms: raw bytes and lowercase
ASCII SHA-256 hex. No uppercase, double-hash, reverse, insertion, token reuse, or
post-hoc variant is allowed.

## Crypto and acceptance

Each registered password is tested against the corrected SalPhaseIon short blob,
the Phase 3.2 small blob, and Cosmic Duality using AES-256-CBC, strict PKCS#7,
and OpenSSL EVP_BytesToKey with each of the two predeclared digests `md5` and
`sha256`.

Padding alone is never success. A single plaintext is accepted only if it passes
the frozen readable-text rule or an exact parser/checksum rule (JSON, gzip, zlib,
ZIP, PNG, PEM, nested OpenSSL envelope, Base58Check, or a canonical hex/Base64
wrapper around readable or exactly parsed content). A cross-blob result requires
the same candidate bytes and same KDF digest to pass one of those validators for
at least two blobs independently.

The machine-readable manifest and its separate SHA-256 seal are generated by:

```text
python -m solver.salphaseion_preregister
```

Only after that seal exists may the blind evaluator run:

```text
python -m solver.salphaseion_blind_eval
```

## Sealed v2 extension

The first sealed round omitted an operation that is justified without reference
to any AES output: an integer “sum list” may itself be a byte list rather than a
printed decimal list. A separate v2 manifest therefore registers raw-byte and
lowercase-hex sum lists, spaced JSON lists, the two page-authenticated modulo-26
alphabet conventions, and the authenticated first-puzzle “last bit” operation.
It does not alter or replace v1, and it inherits the same KDF and acceptance
rules. It must receive its own seal before evaluation.

A final v3 representation-only extension registers the 32 raw SHA-256 digest
bytes for every v1/v2 preimage. The first rounds registered raw preimages and
lowercase hexadecimal SHA-256; v3 resolves the only remaining mechanical
ambiguity in the page's `sha256 ans too` instruction without introducing any
new semantic answer or operation.

The v4 relational extension uses the exact local order `S91`,
`matrixsumlist`, `S570`: S91 row/column sum values directly index the following
S570 field under the page's one- and zero-based conventions. It allows no modulo,
offset, reversal, or fitted reordering. Selected letters, their direct numeric
spellings, and the page's existing decimal-to-hex byte decode are frozen before
decryption, with raw, lowercase SHA-256 hex, and raw SHA-256 digest password
representations.

## v5 adjacent-length matrix rule

The decoded field lengths provide a non-fitted S570 dimension that earlier
rounds missed: `len(lastwordsbeforearchichoice) + len(thispassword) = 26 + 12 =
38`, while `570 = 15 x 38`. This parallels `len(matrixsumlist) = 13` and `91 =
7 x 13`. v5 therefore registers only 13-entry S91 and 38-entry S570 row/column
sum vectors, both row-major orientations, direct canonical serializations,
stable adjacent-key ordering, sum-with-key modulo 26, and S91+S570 source-order
concatenation. No Cosmic-derived material is used.

## S-field statistical classification

The archived source fields were compared with the two controls that actually
decode through the decimal-number route. The chi-square tests use the alphabet
present in each encoding: ten symbols and 9 degrees of freedom for Z1/Z2, but
nine symbols and 8 degrees of freedom for S91/S570.

| Field | Length | Chi-square | df | p-value | IC | `o` count |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Z1 | 63 | 8.2698 | 9 | 0.5072 | 0.098822 | 3 |
| Z2 | 29 | 14.1034 | 9 | 0.1187 | 0.118227 | 7 |
| S91 | 91 | 40.2418 | 8 | 2.89e-6 | 0.150916 | 0 |
| S570 | 570 | 43.7368 | 8 | 6.38e-7 | 0.118090 | 0 |

Under an ordinary uniform decimal-digit model, omitting zero from 570 positions
has probability `0.9^570 = 8.28e-27`. This strongly rejects treating S570 as the
same kind of decimal numeral as Z1/Z2 and retires whole-field decimal/base
conversion as a primary hypothesis.

The skew alone does not prove natural-language ciphertext. Conditional shuffle
tests preserve each field's symbol counts. S570 adjacent mutual information and
trigram repeats are unexceptional (`p=0.247` and `p=0.743` one-sided). Period 13
for S91 column IC (`p=0.014`) and lag 38 for S570 mutual information (`p=0.035`)
are individually suggestive, but a familywise route test over row, column,
serpentine, and spiral readings gives `p=0.368` for S91 and `p=0.374` for S570.
The working classification is therefore **structured nine-symbol data**, with
enciphered text still a hypothesis rather than an authenticated fact.

## v6 Z9 matrix-sum rule

The S-field classification motivates a distinct algebraic construction rather
than another decimal/base conversion. v6 treats `a..i` as the nine elements of
`Z/9Z`, computes only the 13-entry and 38-entry sum vectors aligned to the
adjacent decoded fields, and serializes results as nine-symbol strings. Both
page-supported residue conventions (`a=0` and `a=1, i=0`) are frozen. Direct
vectors, stable adjacent-key order, addition of adjacent A0Z25/A1Z26 key values
modulo 9, and corresponding S91+S570 source-order concatenations are admitted.
Raw, lowercase SHA-256 hex, and raw SHA-256 digest password representations use
the existing blind acceptance rules.

## v18-v22 missed-connection audit

Five further sealed rounds test relationships that were absent from the first
ledger:

- v18 reads `lastwordsbeforearchichoice` as the Architect's final utterances
  around Neo's actual door choice, including every suffix of the Hope line and
  the two transcript timing boundaries.
- v19 treats untouched S91, rather than a speculative permutation of it, as
  seven 13-symbol passwords and fixes concatenation, column interleaving, and
  XOR of seven SHA-256 digests.
- v20 uses the exact identity `570 = 6*91 + 24`: S91 and the first 546 S570
  symbols make seven 91-symbol rows, while the 24-symbol remainder aligns with
  the 24 one-based primes through 91. It sums columns modulo nine and either
  adds or replaces the remainder at prime positions.
- v21 closes the omitted Logic-song progression by testing the exact red/black
  rose alternatives and `The Judgement`, prompted independently by the later
  creator rose hint.
- v22 applies the v9 adjacent-key add/subtract rules and then performs the
  previously omitted whole-stream base-9-to-bytes conversion before password
  hashing.

All five are negative under the same blind acceptance gate: 5,706 decryptions,
29 strict-padding hits, no readable or exactly parsed plaintext, and no rule
validating two blobs.

Two direct non-AES identifications were also falsified. An exhaustive T9 test
over all nine possible separator symbols and every `8!` key assignment covers

## Whole-plaintext cross-stage validation

The original blind evaluator required one password to open two blobs. That
missed a distinct reading of the page's explicit `shabefanstoo` instruction:
an opaque first decryption might itself be an answer whose whole plaintext must
be SHA-256 hashed before entering another blob. This rule was sealed separately
before evaluation (manifest SHA-256
`cc6571881715996463ca45dc9f58ead74b79ce30c169b8dd4b9e3070f21f55d3`).

Every strict-padding plaintext from all v1-v22 candidates was carried forward
without inspection or splitting. Its complete raw bytes, SHA-256 lowercase hex,
and SHA-256 raw digest were tested as passwords for each other independently
salted blob under both supported KDF digests. The first stage made 71,184
attempts and retained all 253 padding hits. The frozen rule then made 3,036
second-stage attempts: 11 passed padding, but none was readable or matched an
exact predeclared format. Status: `NO_ACCEPTED_CROSS_STAGE_RESULT`.

This closes the missing staged grammar without granting binary noise meaning
after the fact. The reproducible files are
`salphaseion_cross_stage_preregistered.json`,
`salphaseion_cross_stage_preregistered.sha256`, and
`salphaseion_cross_stage_results.json`.

## v23-v27 overlooked source-layout connections

Returning to physical token order exposed exact relationships not represented
by v1-v22:

- The 104 raw `a/b` bits spelling `matrixsumlist` equal `pi(570)`, the number
  of one-based prime positions in the immediately following S570 field. v23
  aligns those streams and tests prime extraction, parity/XOR, Base-9 bytes,
  and the two natural 13-entry sum lists. Its AES subset is negative: 2,412
  attempts, eight padding hits, zero accepted plaintexts.
- The stronger completed layout is `91+104=195=15x13`, directly followed by
  `570=15x38`. The widths are exactly `len(matrixsumlist)=13` and
  `len(lastwordsbeforearchichoice)+len(thispassword)=26+12=38`. v24 tests the
  two 15-row column-sum lists and length-matched key arithmetic: 16,704
  attempts, 63 padding hits, zero accepted.
- The second matrix splits in source order as `38=13+13+12`, matching the
  26-character `lastwords...` field and 12-character `thispassword`. v25 tests
  the shared 15-row lists and elementary sums/differences of the equal 15x13
  blocks: 11,070 attempts, 39 padding hits, zero accepted.
- v26 corrects an incomplete transcript hypothesis. It registers every suffix
  before both Architect-spoken occurrences of `choice` in the verified scene,
  including the literal endings `given a` and `of the`: 2,520 attempts, nine
  padding hits, zero accepted.
- v27 tests the canonical 3x3 Lo Shu magic square because the source alphabet
  has exactly nine symbols and the decoded instruction literally names a
  matrix sum. All eight symmetries and inverse mappings are fixed, with
  Base-9, decimal-to-hex, and source-supported sum lists: 13,824 attempts, 73
  padding hits, zero accepted.

v23's first direct-output validator was malformed: generated modular-letter
strings automatically satisfied its one-word rule, while generated JSON lists
were valid JSON by construction. Its 80 nominal direct accepts are therefore
tautologies and are explicitly rejected; none is recognizable language, and
the independently valid AES portion remains negative. This is recorded rather
than silently tightening the gate after seeing output.

The whole-plaintext `SHA256 ans too` stage was then sealed for the new work.
v23-v26 made 32,706 first-stage attempts (119 padding hits) and 1,428 automatic
second-stage attempts (four padding hits), with zero accepted results. v27 made
13,824 first-stage attempts (73 padding hits) and 876 automatic second-stage
attempts (five padding hits), also with zero accepted results. These tests use
the complete unmodified first plaintext and never split or inspect it before
the second stage.

These exact source-layout coincidences are the best newly connected dots. They
are likely relevant to the intended construction, but none yet authenticates
the missing operation that turns them into the short-blob password.

### Correction: the 15-row completion is retired

The paragraph above preserved the dimensional identity but still gave it too
much semantic weight. Exact source syntax supplies a stronger control:
`enter` occurs after the 64th Base64 character and instructs the line break
between the two 64-character halves. It is metadata embedded between data
segments, not payload or password material. The 104 `a/b` symbols spelling
`matrixsumlist` occupy the same syntactic role between S91 and S570. Appending
those instruction bits to S91 to manufacture `91+104=15x13` therefore mixes a
delimiter with its operand. The arithmetic identity remains true but is no
longer an admissible matrix construction.

The corrected grammar keeps S91 at its natural `7x13` size, removes both
instruction markers from their neighboring data, and reads the two-door line
literally. Two missed transcript families were sealed first: every suffix of
`As you adequately put, the problem is` (v33: 756 attempts, zero accepted) and
Neo's exact preceding words `Choice. The problem is choice.` (v34: 1,026
attempts, zero accepted). v35 then applies those exact answers as modular keys
to the untouched S fields using the page-established numeric conversions
(9,720 attempts, zero accepted; its 105 nominal direct hits are bare numbers
accepted as JSON and are rejected tautologies). Finally, v36 derives the row
and column sum lists from S91's actual 7x13 matrix, uses them to key S570 before
or after the Architect answer, and hashes only the resulting candidate answer
(58,860 attempts, 232 padding hits, zero accepted).

This retires direct AES enumeration from matrix material. The live frontier is
to identify the cipher or indexing operation represented by S91/S570 before
AES is involved. No authenticated clue currently selects a further arithmetic
or serialization rule.
